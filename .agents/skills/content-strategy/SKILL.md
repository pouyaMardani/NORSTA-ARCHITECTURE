# Content Strategy

## Understand the Project

- Read the project brief, roadmap, brand guidelines, and existing content.
- Identify the target audience and the primary purpose of the website.
- Understand the difference between educational content, portfolio content, and conversion-focused content.
- Preserve established business requirements and content decisions.
- Ask questions only when missing information blocks a meaningful decision.

## Audience and Search Intent

- Identify the questions users are trying to answer.
- Match each page to a clear user need.
- Distinguish informational, navigational, and commercial investigation intent when relevant.
- Avoid creating multiple pages that target the same intent without a clear purpose.
- Prioritize usefulness and clarity over keyword repetition.

## Content Architecture

- Organize content into clear topics and categories.
- Establish relationships between articles, portfolio projects, and relevant service information.
- Use consistent naming conventions for categories and content types.
- Avoid creating unnecessary categories with little or no useful content.
- Make important content accessible through navigation and contextual internal links.
- Keep the content structure manageable as the website grows.

## Editorial Standards

- Write clear, accurate, helpful, and audience-appropriate content.
- Use consistent terminology throughout the website.
- Break long articles into descriptive sections.
- Use headings, lists, examples, and captions when they improve readability.
- Avoid repetitive paragraphs and generic filler.
- Do not invent technical claims, project details, customer stories, credentials, or testimonials.
- Mark unverified information for review instead of presenting it as fact.
- Do not publish placeholder content as finished editorial content.

## Portfolio Content

- Treat portfolio projects as evidence of actual work.
- Use owner-approved photographs and verified project details.
- Record available information such as project type, materials, finish, location, and completion date only when verified.
- Do not assume that every field is known for every project.
- Write descriptive captions that explain the visible work without exaggeration.
- Avoid presenting stock or generated imagery as completed client projects.

## Educational Content

- Explain concepts in a logical order, starting with what readers need to know.
- Define technical terms when they first appear.
- Distinguish established facts from opinions, recommendations, and project-specific experiences.
- Verify technical claims using trustworthy sources when necessary.
- Keep content updated when materials, methods, or relevant guidance change.
- Avoid unsupported claims about durability, waterproofing, safety, or maintenance.

## Content Planning

For each proposed page or article, identify:

- Its purpose.
- Its intended audience.
- Its primary topic.
- Its search intent when relevant.
- Its relationship to existing pages.
- Its main call to action, if applicable.
- The information or assets still needed.

Do not create a content calendar or large batch of articles unless the project requires it.

## Internal Linking

- Link related articles and portfolio projects where useful.
- Use descriptive anchor text.
- Avoid excessive or repetitive links.
- Ensure links lead to relevant, existing destinations.
- Identify orphaned important pages when reviewing the site.

## Verification

- Check factual accuracy and consistency.
- Review headings, links, metadata, and image captions.
- Verify that content fulfills its stated purpose.
- Check spelling, readability, and supported languages.
- Identify missing owner-provided information before publication.

## Completion Standard

Content should be useful, accurate, well-organized, consistent with the brand, and ready for its intended audience. Unverified claims and missing source material must be clearly identified.
